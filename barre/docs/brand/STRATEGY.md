# Brand strategy and positioning (Phase two)

Written as the shared strategic foundation for all three creative concepts. Concept-specific narrative
and voice are on each concept board. "Audience" insights are the team's professional judgement from the
dance-industry and user-experience perspectives, not research with real users.

## The insight
Dance schools are run by artists. Most owners started as teachers; the admin — registers, invoices,
chasing forms, timetable changes, safeguarding records — eats the evenings that should go to teaching
and choreography. Existing tools look like school-office software, so the business side of a creative
school feels unlike the school itself. Families, meanwhile, live in WhatsApp threads and lost emails,
and students rarely see their own progress between grade exams.

## Purpose
Give dance schools more time for dance.

## Vision
Every dance school runs as smoothly as its best rehearsal.

## Proposition
One place for the timetable, the register, the money and the messages — and the part nobody else
offers: each student's progress, told to them and their family. The structure is handled so people can
teach, learn and perform.

## Promise
The admin gets done, quietly and correctly. Nobody is left out of the loop.

## Personality
Confident · warm · exact · energetic · unpretentious.
Creative without chaos; playful without being childish; professional without being corporate.

## Values
- **Craft** — get the detail right, every time (money, times, names, medical information).
- **Care** — safeguarding and privacy by design; no private staff–child channels; children's data kept small.
- **Momentum** — progress is visible and celebrated without pressure or rankings.
- **Every style, every age** — ballet to breaking, three-year-olds to adult beginners.

## Audiences
| Who | Buys? | What they need to feel | What wins them |
|---|---|---|---|
| School owner | Yes | In control; proud of how the school looks | Time back; money collected; a product that looks like their school is serious |
| Office administrator | Influences | Calm on Monday morning | Fewer clicks; one list of what needs doing |
| Teacher | Uses daily | Fast; trusted | Register in seconds; feedback in minutes; no admin in the studio |
| Student (teen/adult) | Uses | Seen; motivated | Their own progress, goals and milestones; kind, specific feedback |
| Parent | Uses; can churn a school | Reassured; informed | Each child's week, notices that matter, simple payment |

## Functional benefits
Timetable and changes with families told automatically; one-tap registers with medical markers;
term and monthly billing with discounts; enrolment from enquiry to class; shows, exams and events;
progress, feedback and goals; safeguarding log; one login across schools.

## Emotional benefits
Owners: pride and relief. Teachers: respected time. Students: recognition. Parents: trust.

## Differentiation (verified competitor positions from the earlier market review)
ClassForKids, Class Manager, DanceBiz, Jackrabbit Dance and DanceStudio-Pro centre on booking, billing
and admin; Class Manager shows parents the current grade only. None found shows students and families an
ongoing story of progress, or lets each school appear in its own style. Visual quality across the
category is functional and dated (assessment from vendor sites and reviews, not a formal study).

## Narrative
Every dance has a structure underneath: counts, marks, spacing, a running order. Nobody in the audience
sees it, but without it nothing works. [Brand] is that structure for a dance school — the timetable, the
register, the money, the messages — kept exact and out of the way, so the people in the studio can do
what they came for: teach, learn and perform.

## Voice
Plain, warm, specific. Short sentences. Name real things (classes, children, rooms, amounts). Encourage
without gushing; never blame. Office voice: calm and exact. Teacher voice: brisk. Student voice:
encouraging, specific. Parent voice: reassuring, complete.

## Messaging hierarchy
1. More time for dance.
2. Everything the school runs on, in one place.
3. Progress students and families can see.
4. Safe by design.
5. Looks like your school.

## Tagline candidates
"More time for dance." · "Five, six, seven, eight." · "Everything in its place." · "Room to grow."
