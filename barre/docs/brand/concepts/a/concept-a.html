02

Strategy

Sotto comes from sotto voce: in a low voice. On every stage, coloured spike tape marks where people and things go — small, exact, understood by every dancer in every style. Sotto is that tape for the whole school: it puts everything on its mark and stays quiet, so the dance is what people hear.

Minimum size

Wordmark 64px wide on screen (18mm print). Below that, the spike tape drops out and the mark or app icon is used instead.

Clear space

Keep clear one o-height (the x-height) on every side. Nothing sits on the tape strip.

Don’t

Re-colour the tape outside yellow, white or graphite. Straighten it. Set “sotto” in a font. Add a second strip.

Colour

Rehearsal-room daylight: pale porcelain walls, white surfaces, a graphite mirror frame. Marley, the blue-grey vinyl of the studio floor, does the work. Spike yellow is the tape: chosen because real yellow spike tape is the one colour that reads on both black stages and grey Marley, and it is vivid without being fluorescent.

Brand colour rule

One spike mark per view region, at most: the current item, the “now” line, the active nav item, a checked-in dancer. Marley is reserved for the single primary action on a screen. Everything else is graphite on white.

Status rule

Semantic colours only mean state, and always travel with a word and a shape: square = critical, triangle = warning, circle = info, tick = done. Yellow is never a warning.

Graphic language

The floor is the grid. Information sits on a strict, quiet 12-column grid with generous air, and a short strip of tape marks the one thing that matters right now.

Strip — current, selected, checked in
Cross — a fixed point: the mark, the logo
Long strip — the “now” line
Coloured strip — dance style, with name

Torn ends, a 2–6° tilt, never perfectly square. One mark per region.

Concept notes

Strengths

  • The motif is functional, not decorative: a tape strip already means “here / this one” to every dancer, so it doubles as the selection and “now” indicator.
  • Style-neutral. Spike tape is on ballet stages and street battle floors alike; nothing here reads as a children’s club.
  • Very low colour load in the product: graphite on white, one Marley button, one yellow mark. Semantic colours stay unambiguous.
  • The name carries the product promise (quiet, underneath) without saying “admin”.

Weaknesses

  • Restraint can read as plain. Without the tape, screens could be any well-made SaaS; the motif has to be applied consistently to earn recognition.
  • The tape cross is close to a “+” at small sizes; next to “add” buttons or medical marks it could be misread. Hence medical markers use dots and squares, never crosses.
  • “Sotto” needs saying once to be understood; some will hear “so-tto” as just a name.

Risks

  • Yellow tape fails contrast as text and on white is a graphical accent only (about 1.7:1). Teams will be tempted to use it for buttons or warnings — the rule must be policed.
  • The torn-edge clip-path, overused, turns into a gimmick. One mark per region.
  • Name availability (domains, trademarks in software class) unchecked.

Implementation complexity — Low to medium

  • Two Google fonts, flat colour, no imagery dependency: low.
  • The tape is one CSS clip-path utility plus one SVG; motion is two easing tokens.
  • Medium only for discipline: a strict grid and density rules across many screens, and photography that must be commissioned (floors from above) rather than bought.
