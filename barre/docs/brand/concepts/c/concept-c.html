02

Strategy

A school is a place where people grow — a first plié, a first battle, a first show. Every movement has an arc, and so does a year of classes. Solemi draws that arc for each student and carries the admin quietly underneath it, so a school's energy goes into room to grow.

The rule

Ever-green marks the one thing to do next — a single primary action per view and the active navigation item. Apricot is reserved for the arc: the landing dot, milestones, a drawn progress line. 06

Graphic language

One shape doing four jobs. If it isn't an arc, it's a straight, quiet line.

The arc as mark

A jump's flight, a port de bras, a body wave. Thin at take-off, weighted at landing. Never rotated, never doubled, never a smile.

The arc as progress

Development drawn as an arc that grows: a term, a goal, a level. It only ever fills forward. "Not yet" is an empty track, never red.

The arc as divider

A gentle 1.25 px rise separates major moments — the top of a marketing page, the end of a celebration. Inside product screens, plain rules do the work.

Icons

24 px grid, 1.75 px stroke (2.1 when active), round caps and joins, open forms, no fills. Icons label navigation and status only — never decorate a heading.

Motion

Arcs draw on once — stroke-dashoffset, 400–600 ms, ease-in-out — then the landing dot settles. Only for progress and celebrations. Everything else is quick (120 ms fades) and still. With reduced motion, arcs appear fully drawn.

560 ms · cubic-bezier(.65,0,.35,1) · dot pops in 260 ms on landing.

07

Product

People first. Student and parent screens are personal — one warm ground, one arc, large readable type. Teacher screens are fast. Owner screens are calm and organised, with rules instead of boxes.

08

Marketing

Photography leads: real rehearsal moments — hands, feet, mid-air, warm light, every age and style. The arc is drawn over the image, following the movement in the shot. Placeholders below are art-direction briefs, not photos.

09

Concept notes

An honest read, for comparing against A and B.

Strengths

  • One motif that genuinely does work: logo, progress, icon and divider are the same shape.
  • Progress-as-arc makes "developing" feel like a trajectory, not a grade — right for students and parents.
  • Style-neutral: an arc is a jump, a wave, a port de bras. Works for street as well as ballet.
  • Colour discipline keeps owner screens calm; warmth is concentrated where people are.

Weaknesses

  • An arc with a dot is a familiar shape; ownership depends on the tapered weight and the l-to-i landing.
  • Owner screens are quiet to the point of plain; the brand is mostly felt on personal screens.
  • Apricot can't carry text, so warmth on white relies on shape, not words.

Risks

  • Marketing depends on commissioned photography; stock imagery would make it generic fast.
  • Arc gauges could creep into every metric — limit them to progress and celebrations.
  • "Solemi" needs trademark and domain clearance; pronunciation may drift (so-LEE-mee).
  • Mint and sky grounds can read pastel and young if overused.

Implementation

Medium

  • Tokens, two Google fonts and an SVG icon set: low effort.
  • Arc progress components (segmented levels, gauges, draw-on) need one careful shared component.
  • The photography programme is the real cost — budget for a shoot across styles and ages.
