02 — Strategy

What movement leaves behind

Marey and Muybridge photographed motion as a row of overlapping exposures: the body, a moment ago and now. Swayvo borrows that idea. The admin keeps a quiet record so that progress, attendance and growth become visible as echoes, while the screen people actually work on stays still.

Personality

Poised. Expressive. Assured. Quietly bold.

Tone of voice: plain, adult, warm without exclamation marks

  • Owner alertThree card payments didn't go through, £162.00 in total. Retry them now, or send each family a short note.
  • Teacher promptJunior Tap is done. One line for Layla's family about her trial?
  • StudentContraction and release has moved to secure. That's your work, remembered.
  • Parent noticeGrade 2 Ballet on Tuesday 1 December moves to Studio Two. Same time, same teacher.
03 — Identity

A name that moves

A condensed, sharply cut lowercase wordmark drawn from scratch. Lowercase keeps the name conversational and puts every letter in one x-height band, so the echo of the v reads as motion rather than as a capital shouting. The thick-thin diagonals borrow from high-contrast fashion type; the v is the only letter that moves, stepping forward in two fading exposures to land in Safelight.

Clear space

Keep clear space equal to the width of the o on all sides. The echoes count as part of the logo: never crop or crowd them.

Minimum size

Wordmark: 96px / 24mm wide minimum with echoes; down to 72px in one-colour. Below that, use the small mark.

Don't
  • Echo any letter other than the v, or add a third echo.
  • Reverse the echo direction: time moves left to right.
  • Set "Swayvo" in a font as a substitute for the drawn mark.
  • Put Safelight on anything but the present v.
04 — Colour

Silver print, one safelight

The palette of a gelatin silver print: a cool silver-white ground, carbon blacks and a long grey ramp that does the echo work. The single jewel is Safelight, a deep oxblood taken from the darkroom lamp under which photographs appear. It is unusual in software, adult rather than playful, and owned by none of the obvious dance-app blues and pinks.

The rule

Safelight appears once per screen: on the one action that moves the day forward, or on the marker for "now". Everything else is Carbon on Gelatin. If a second thing wants the colour, one of them is wrong.

06 — Graphic language

The echo, used rarely

One form, repeated in two or three offset, fading exposures, always stepping left to right: where it was, where it is now. It appears in the logo, on progress, in transitions and on marketing type. Everyday UI stays perfectly still.

The echo rule Steps are solid tints (#D4D4CF, #A3A39E), not transparency, so overlaps stay clean on any ground. In one-colour use they become two strokes of decreasing weight.
Where the echo is allowed
  • Logo · the v, always.
  • Progress · a skill's earlier levels trail behind the current one.
  • Transitions · a new page title arrives with two trails, then holds.
  • Marketing type · one word per layout, never a whole line.
  • Never · on buttons, lists, registers, money, alerts.
Icons · 24px grid, 1.5px stroke, square caps, no fills
Icons sit in navigation and controls only, never beside headings. The Progress icon is the only one that echoes.
Motion · echo on arrival
securesecuresecure

An element arrives over 240ms (ease-out) with two fading trails, then holds completely still. Used on page titles and when a skill level changes. With reduced motion turned on: no trails, no travel, the new state simply appears.

07 — Product

Still screens, one moving idea

Magazine-header titles for owners, students and parents; fast, plain lists for teachers; perfectly aligned tables for operations. Very few containers: rules and whitespace do the grouping.

Why the screens look like this

  • Register is the plainest screen in the product: 56px rows, separate In / Out targets of 48px, medical notes in words with a cross or a critical diamond, and the trial dancer named in text.
  • Progress is the only place the echo touches the UI: the current level in Safelight with Contraction and release's earlier position trailing behind. Developing is a position on a line, not a red mark.
  • Parent and payment stay calm and exact: tabular figures, a single total, one Safelight button. The cancellation gets the critical diamond and a reason.
  • Safelight is used once per screen: Send link, Open register, the "now" level, Pay.

09 — Concept notes

Honestly

Strengths

  • The echo gives progress a visual meaning no competitor has: you can see where a skill was.
  • Monochrome plus one jewel looks premium and works for ballet, street and commercial alike.
  • The UI is mostly still and plain, so the teacher register stays fast.
  • Photography direction travels from a hip-hop battle to a Grade 1 class without changing.

Weaknesses

  • Very little colour: style coding in the timetable relies on line patterns, which are subtler than colour.
  • The condensed wordmark needs care below 96px; the small mark carries the brand on phones.
  • Parents of young children may find it cool rather than warm.

Risks

  • The echo is easy to overuse; one extra trail turns gallery into motion-blur cliché.
  • Safelight and critical are both in the red family; the shape-plus-word rule must be enforced in code.
  • The look depends on real black-and-white photography; a budget shoot will undersell it.

Complexity · medium

  • Tokens and components: low. Neutral palette, two Google fonts, square controls.
  • Echo motion: medium. One shared CSS/FLIP utility, reduced-motion fallback built in.
  • Photography: medium to high, a commissioned b/w library across eight styles.
