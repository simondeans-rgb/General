02 / Strategy

The board in the corridor

The idea

Every dance school runs on paper on a wall: the show poster, the battle flyer, the recital programme, the "class moved" notice. Swayvo takes that culture of print — two vivid riso inks that overprint into a third — and makes it the school's shared noticeboard.

Loud and inky on posters, celebrations and empty states. Inside the product the press calms right down: flat ink on clean paper, so registers, invoices and timetables stay exact.

03 / Identity

Two inks, one stamp

Hand-built poster lettering: heavy, compact, flat-topped strokes cut to the x-height like wood type. One cut makes it ours — the y drops and runs under "vo" as a printer's swash. The wordmark prints in teal; a pink disc behind the "o" overprints to navy. The mark is the same move on the s.

The ink rule

One ink per screen for the main action. Owner and teacher screens use teal; parent and student screens use pink. Navy is the overprint — it appears where the two inks meet (logo, celebrations, dark grounds), never as a third button colour.

Inks are for marks, big moments and graphics, not text. Text is ink-black, or text-weight teal for links. Status always uses the semantic set, always with a shape and a word — a cancelled class is never "just pink".

Grain and misregistration are print effects. Posters, onboarding, milestones, empty states. Never on data, never on body text.

2 · The notice

In the product, changes get type, not boxes: a squeezed uppercase verb (MOVED, CANCELLED, DUE), a mono date stamp, the detail in plain Work Sans, a short heavy rule. No cards, no badges.

Reduced motion: the inks simply appear aligned.

3 · Registration (motion)

Confirmations and celebrations: the second ink slides 4 px into register over 200 ms (ease-out). Everything else is instant — no page transitions, no bouncing. prefers-reduced-motion removes the slide.

09 / Concept notes

Honestly

Strengths

  • Rooted in something every dance school already does — print posters, flyers and notices — so it feels like the culture, not software.
  • Pink × teal → navy gives three brand colours for the price of two, and a rule (overprint) that anyone at the school can apply on a flyer.
  • Works across ballet and street: the gig-poster register is credible for battles, the programme register for recitals.
  • A clear on/off switch for expression: loud outside and at milestones, calm and flat inside. The product screens stay fast and exact.

Weaknesses

  • Fluorescent pink cannot be reproduced on screen or in CMYK — #FF48B0 is a stand-in; true fluoro needs riso or spot-colour print.
  • The y-swash wordmark is wide; below ~88 px it must hand over to the mark.
  • Pink reads young to some audiences if it creeps into the UI — the ink rule has to be policed.

Risks

  • Riso is a fashionable look in indie design; it could date. Mitigation: the system rests on the overprint rule and the notice typography, not on grain.
  • Teachers and owners may want more colour-coding than the "one ink per screen" rule allows. Style tints are the release valve.
  • Duotone photography depends on schools supplying good photos with consent — needs a simple in-app duotone tool and a consent flow.
  • Pink/teal can collide with semantic red/blue if misused; semantics always carry a shape and a word.

Implementation complexity · Medium

Product UI is low effort: flat colours, one variable font (Anybody) plus Work Sans, no shadows or radii. Medium overall because of the print layer: CSS multiply blends and grain need fallbacks, the logo needs careful export (masks/clips for the overprint), and marketing needs a duotone photo pipeline.
