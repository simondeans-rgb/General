02 · STRATEGY

The eight-count

Ballet, tap, breaking, ballroom — every style counts music in eights. Swayvo turns that shared count into a system: an eight-beat bar that is the logo, the progress meter, the loader and the rhythm of the week. The admin keeps time in the background so teachers and dancers can stay on the music.

03 · IDENTITY

Wordmark, beat mark, icon

The wordmark is drawn from a single 14-unit stroke on a 56-unit x-height, leaning 10° forward. The mark is one bar of eight beats: a strong "1", a lifted "5", grouped in fours like counting out loud.

Clear space

Keep clear space equal to the height of a short beat (x) on every side. Nothing enters it — not even the school's own logo.

Minimum size

Primary lockup: 96 px / 22 mm wide. Wordmark alone: 64 px / 15 mm. Below 24 px use the mark; below 20 px use the pixel-fitted favicon.

The rule

In the app, Count blue marks one primary action per screen and the now-beat (the next class, today's column, the current week). Everything else is Ink on White. Status is never brand blue and never colour alone — each state carries a shape (◆ ▲ ● ○) and a word. Style colours are 4-px marks beside the style's name, nothing more.

Motion · on the beat

One beat = 120 ms (an allegro 125 bpm). Lists enter in staggered fours; loaders light beats in time; success is the bar filling to 8 and holding. Nothing bounces or spins. With reduced motion, the bar is simply full and lists appear at once.

09 · CONCEPT NOTES

Honestly

Strengths
  • One idea does real work: the beat bar is logo, meter, loader, nav state and success — the system explains itself.
  • The eight-count belongs to every style equally; nothing reads ballet-first or children's-club.
  • Loud brand, quiet product: blue is earned (sign-in, milestones, marketing), so data screens stay readable.
  • Big expanded numerals make "what's next / what's owed" scannable between classes.
Weaknesses
  • Bar glyphs can read as a signal-strength or audio-level icon at a glance; the grouped 4+4 rhythm and peach downbeat have to carry the difference.
  • "Beats" as a progress unit is abstract — 8 is not a percentage, so meters need numbers beside them.
  • Archivo Expanded eats width; long class names need the 112% cut in lists.
Risks
  • Sporty energy could feel pushy for calm ballet schools and anxious parents — tone of voice must stay encouraging, never Strava-competitive.
  • Skill meters risk being read as grades; "developing" must never look like failure (no red, no empty-state shame).
  • Count blue is close to many SaaS blues; the peach downbeat and the wordmark lean are what make it ownable.
Implementation complexity

Medium

Two Google variable fonts, a small token set and one reusable <Beats n= of=> component cover most of the UI. Motion is CSS-only. The cost is discipline: guarding the blue budget and keeping the motif from spreading into decoration.
